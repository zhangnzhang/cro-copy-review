## 1. Most-used test

| Who | Change | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veeam Software | Product-page link "Request a quote" → "Request pricing" | Click-through to the sales inquiry page 0.54% → 1.40% (+161.66%) | [VWO case study (PDF)](https://static.wingify.com/vwo/uploads/2019/06/veeam-software-increased-conversions_2019-06-29-19-30-54.pdf) |

How to use it:
> "Veeam changed 'Request a quote' to 'Request pricing' and click-through rose 161% — people knew exactly what they'd get. Your 'Learn more' has the same problem; try 'Watch the 60-second demo'."

Note that it measured clicks, not purchases, on a B2B site. Use it to support **clarity of CTA wording**, not as a promise of a sales lift.

## 2. Six likeability boosters

Use 1–2 of these when Emotional Resonance scores low.

1. **Storytelling** — a real or imagined situation the reader steps into. Move from the product to a person. *Good for:* brand story, About page, origin story, testimonials.
2. **Painting a picture** — vivid language that creates an image. Not "beautiful" but "brushed brass that catches the morning light". *Good for:* premium and sensory products (food, scent, fabric).
3. **Advertising tone** — rhythm and energy that build anticipation; a little over the top, never cringe. *Good for:* launches, seasonal campaigns, young brands.
4. **Endorsements** — users, experts, media. Not persuasion: proof others already chose this. *Good for:* everything.
5. **Metaphors and similes** — make the abstract tangible. Not "stable structure" but "steady as bedrock". *Good for:* tech, abstract services, new categories.
6. **Humor** — playful or self-deprecating warmth that makes it feel less salesy. *Careful with:* medical, legal, financial and other high-trust categories.

## 3. PAS in practice

**Problem → Agitation → Solution.**

1. **Problem** — one concrete situation, not an abstract pain.
2. **Agitation** — dig in until the reader thinks "yes, that's me".
3. **Solution** — how the product makes it stop.

**Example (a fictional standing desk):**
> **P:** By 3pm your lower back is louder than your inbox.
> **A:** You've tried the lumbar pillow, the stretches, the reminder app. You're still folded over a laptop eight hours a day.
> **S:** The [Desk name] goes from sitting to standing in 4 seconds, so you can switch every hour without breaking focus.

PAS doesn't have to be three separate paragraphs. Look for the logical flow, not the layout.

## 4. Catchy intro formula

Popularized by Derek Halpern (Social Triggers). Works for blog posts, long-form heroes and email intros.

1. **Benefit-led title** — promise the outcome.
2. **A question that builds interest.**
3. **A promise to close the loop** — say what the reader will get.

> **Title:** 7 habits that make running shoes last twice as long
> **Question:** Ever retired a pair at 300 miles and wondered where they went?
> **Promise:** Here are the 7 habits long-distance runners swear by, including the one most beginners get wrong.

## 5. Making complex information readable

Adapted from Roy Peter Clark's advice on complex information in *Writing Tools*. Use when copy has to explain something technical.

1. Use the shortest, most common words.
2. Use fewer numbers, and give each one context.
3. Cut secondary numbers.
4. Round big numbers ($2,632,024 → $2.6 million).
5. Put heavy data in a chart or table.
6. Slow down at the hard part — give the key sentence its own line.
7. The more complex the idea, the shorter the words, sentences and paragraphs.

## 6. "It's like X, but for Y"

A reliable way to make a new idea click.

- Ketones: like glucose, but made from fat.
- Backlinks: like votes Google uses to judge a site.
- "Uber for X" — a whole genre of startup pitches.

**Try-outs for common e-commerce products:**
- "It's like a jewelry box, but for your tools."
- "It's like a personal trainer, but for your posture."
- "The Swiss Army knife of carry-on bags."

Suggest one when dimension 10 scores ≤ 2 and the product is new or abstract.

## 7. Feature → benefit library

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| 2000W motor | The whole house in 12 minutes |
| HEPA 13 filter | Keeps allergens out of your air |
| 304 stainless steel | Still rust-free in ten years |
| AI object detection | It learns the room; you don't have to |
| Reinforced stitching | Survives the overhead bin, trip after trip |
| Waterproof coating | Spill coffee on it; wipe it off |
| 6-hour battery | Living room to upstairs without recharging |
| Double-wall insulation | Coffee still hot at your 11am meeting |

*Numbers are illustrative — use only figures you can stand behind.*

**Conversion patterns:**
- "Made of X / has X" → "lasts N years / won't / lets you"
- "Smart / automatic / AI" → "you don't have to do X anymore"
- "High quality" → delete it; replace with a concrete guarantee (warranty, returns, materials)
- "Stylish design" → "the thing people ask you about"

## 8. CTA library

### Lead gen / newsletter
| ❌ Weak | ✅ Stronger |
|---|---|
| Submit | Get my free quote |
| Subscribe | Subscribe — one email a week, no spam |
| Sign up | Sign me up — $5 off my first order |
| Click here | Show me my discount |

### E-commerce
| ❌ Weak | ✅ Stronger |
|---|---|
| Buy now | Add to cart — ships today |
| Order | Order now — free shipping over $50 |
| Shop | Shop the travel collection |
| Get | Get my starter kit |

### B2B / SaaS
| ❌ Weak | ✅ Stronger |
|---|---|
| Request a quote | Request pricing (Veeam test, see §1) |
| Learn more | Show me how it works |
| Get started | Start my 14-day free trial |
| Contact us | Talk to a real human |

**Avoid:** "Submit" (mechanical), "Click here" (says nothing), "OK" (unclear), bare "Learn more" (learn, then what?), ALL CAPS and Title Case buttons.

## 9. Vitamin vs painkiller

The question: does the product help over time (vitamin) or solve an immediate problem (painkiller)?

**Vitamins** — skincare, décor, accessories, hobbies, subscriptions, education.
Sell the slow, compounding good: "grows with you", "built to last". Forced urgency backfires.

**Painkillers** — repair tools, emergency supplies, cleaning products, earplugs, pain relief.
Sell the pain disappearing: "never again", "fixed today". Urgency and agitation fit.

If painkiller copy is used on a vitamin product (or the reverse), deduct under Framework Application.

## 10. Anti-patterns

### ❌ Brand-name headline
> "What is Acme Select?"

Nobody who doesn't know the brand wants to find out what it is. **Fix:** say what it does and for whom.

### ❌ Jargon
> "Leveraging cutting-edge AI to empower your workflow with best-in-class solutions."

Every word is one readers have learned to skip. **Fix:** a concrete scene — "The report you build every Friday at 5pm, done in 5 seconds."

### ❌ Title Case button
> [ Get My Free Trial Now ]

Feels formal and pushy. **Fix:** [ Get my free trial ]

### ❌ Fake scarcity
> "Only 2 left in stock!" (there are 5,000)

Get caught once and trust is gone for good; Trust ≤ 4/10. **Fix:** real scarcity only (seasonal, collaborations, genuinely limited runs).

### ❌ Empty praise
> "Our products are crafted with care, premium quality, and loved worldwide."

Zero information. **Fix:** evidence — price, rating with real review count, guarantee.

### ❌ Feature dump
> "2000W motor, HEPA 13, cordless, 5 speeds, lightweight, ergonomic handle."

Benefit ≤ 5/15. **Fix:** translate each feature into what the reader gets.

### ❌ Three emotions at once (mobile)
> "Limited-time deal! The trusted choice! Premium quality! Grab yours now!"

Urgency + reassurance + exclusivity — none lands. **Fix:** pick one emotion and commit.

### ❌ Fails "So what?"
> "Features YKK zippers, a padded laptop sleeve, a luggage strap, and multiple colors."

So what? So what? So what? So what? **Fix:** give each feature an outcome — "YKK zippers **that won't snag when you're rushing through security**".
